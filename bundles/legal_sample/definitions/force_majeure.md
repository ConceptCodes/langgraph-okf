# Definition: Force Majeure

**"Force Majeure"** means any event or circumstance beyond the reasonable control of
the affected party that prevents or delays performance of contractual obligations,
including but not limited to:

1. Acts of God (floods, earthquakes, hurricanes, wildfires).
2. War, armed conflict, terrorist acts, or civil unrest.
3. Government orders, sanctions, embargoes, or changes in applicable law that make
   performance illegal or commercially impracticable.
4. Epidemics or declared public health emergencies.
5. Widespread internet or telecommunications infrastructure outages not caused by
   the affected party's own systems.
6. Power grid failures affecting an entire region or data centre.

## Exclusions

The following do **not** qualify as Force Majeure events:

- Economic downturns, market changes, or Customer budget constraints.
- Labour disputes internal to the affected party (unless caused by an external event).
- Failure of the affected party's own equipment or third-party vendors (unless the
  vendor failure itself is caused by a Force Majeure event).
- Foreseeable risks that a reasonable party should have planned for (e.g., routine
  hardware failure).

## Usage

See [Force Majeure Clause](/contracts/msa/clauses/force_majeure.md) for notice requirements,
mitigation obligations, and termination rights when a Force Majeure event persists for
more than sixty (60) days.
